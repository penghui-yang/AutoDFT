Compute the vibrational (normal-mode) frequencies of an isolated water molecule, which quantify how the O–H bonds stretch and the H–O–H angle bends and can be compared directly to infrared/Raman spectroscopy. Because the system is a free, non-linear molecule in the gas phase, the physically correct spectrum should include six “zero-frequency” modes corresponding to overall translation and rotation of the whole molecule, plus three genuine internal vibrations (symmetric stretch, antisymmetric stretch, and bending). The expected outcome is a list of vibrational frequencies (often in cm⁻¹) and their mode patterns, distinguishing the near-zero rigid-motion modes from the three internal modes. As a cross-check, the same frequencies can be obtained either from numerical displacements or from a linear-response approach, and the meaningful internal frequencies should be robust even if the near-zero modes are not exactly zero due to small numerical force drifts.
