Use density-functional theory to study antiferromagnetic NiO while *forcing* the nickel magnetic moments to point in chosen directions (and, in a stricter test, to also have chosen magnitudes). This is physically important because NiO is a strongly correlated transition‑metal oxide whose magnetic behavior depends sensitively on local spin orientation, and it can also exhibit spin–orbit–related effects such as orbital moments and magnetic anisotropy. The calculation adds an energetic “penalty” when the moments deviate from the target constraints; by repeating the calculation while systematically tightening this penalty, you can verify that the penalty energy scales as expected with the inverse constraint strength. The main outputs are the stabilized local magnetic moments (direction and optionally size), the total energy including the penalty contribution, and a plot/trend demonstrating the predicted penalty‑energy relationship and how efficiently the constraints enforce the desired spin configuration.
