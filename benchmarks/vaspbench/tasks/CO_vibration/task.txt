Compute the vibrational spectrum of an isolated carbon monoxide (CO) molecule by probing how the total energy (or forces) changes when the atoms are displaced slightly around their equilibrium bond length. CO is a simple but chemically important diatomic molecule, so its stretching vibration provides a clear reference for validating molecular bonding and force constants in a first-principles approach. The main expected result is the CO vibrational frequency (typically reported in cm⁻¹, THz, or meV) along with the corresponding normal-mode eigenvector showing the two atoms moving in opposite directions along the bond. This is obtained by evaluating the curvature of the potential-energy surface near equilibrium (a finite-difference approximation to the molecular Hessian/dynamical matrix), which also reveals near-zero “rigid-translation” modes that should not correspond to real vibrations.
