This calculation evaluates how **spin–orbit coupling** influences the preferred direction of magnetization in a **freestanding, single-atom-thick Ni(100) monolayer** (a 2D ferromagnet where reduced symmetry and dimensionality can strongly affect magnetic anisotropy). You run two otherwise identical electronic-structure calculations with the magnetization constrained first **out of the plane** and then **within the plane**, so that any change in total energy comes from spin–orbit–driven anisotropy. The key result is the **magnetocrystalline anisotropy energy (MAE)**, obtained as the energy difference between these two magnetization directions. For this Ni monolayer, the expected outcome is a small MAE on the order of **meV**, indicating an **in-plane easy axis** (the in-plane magnetization is lower in energy by about 1.2 meV).
