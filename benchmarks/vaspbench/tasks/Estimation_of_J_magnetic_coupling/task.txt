Use density-functional theory with a Hubbard-\(U\) correction to estimate the magnetic exchange coupling \(J_2\) in bulk NiO, a classic correlated antiferromagnetic oxide where electron localization on Ni strongly affects magnetism. Compute and compare the total energies of two different spin arrangements—ferromagnetic (all Ni spins aligned) and antiferromagnetic (neighboring Ni spins opposed)—because the energy difference reflects the strength of the 180° Ni–O–Ni superexchange interaction between next-nearest-neighbor Ni atoms (Ni–Ni distance \(\sim 4.17\) Å). Convert this energy difference into the exchange constant using \(J_2 = (E_{\mathrm{FM}} - E_{\mathrm{AFM}})/12\), and repeat for several choices of the effective \(U\) to see how stronger on-site correlation tends to reduce \(J_2\). The expected result is a set of \(J_2\) values (in meV) versus \(U_{\mathrm{eff}}\), which can be directly compared to the experimental estimate \(J_2 \approx 19.01\) meV.
