Compute the electronic density of states (DOS) of an isolated carbon monoxide (CO) molecule to see how its electrons are distributed in energy and which atomic orbitals (C vs O, and s vs p components) form each molecular state. CO is a simple but chemically important heteronuclear diatomic whose bonding involves polarized molecular orbitals and distinct directional p-orbital contributions (along the bond vs perpendicular), making it a good test case for orbital-resolved analysis. The expected result is a total DOS together with a “partial”/projected DOS that separates contributions from carbon and oxygen and distinguishes different p-orbital directions, allowing you to identify which peaks correspond to specific bonding, nonbonding, or antibonding states. This DOS analysis relies on having a converged electronic ground state for the molecule before projecting and plotting the orbital contributions.
