Calculate the electronic ground state of an **isolated oxygen atom** by placing a single O atom in a sufficiently large “empty” periodic box so it does not interact with copies of itself. This is a simple but important reference calculation because atomic energies and orbital levels are the building blocks for interpreting bonding, reaction energetics, and adsorption energies in molecules and solids. Oxygen is special in that it has partially filled, degenerate \(p\) orbitals, which can lead to fractional orbital occupations and an artificial “electronic entropy” contribution unless handled carefully; physically, you want the **zero‑temperature internal energy** of the atom rather than a finite‑temperature free energy. The expected results are the converged **total energy (especially the zero‑temperature energy)**, the **orbital eigenvalues/occupations** showing the \(p\)-level degeneracy, and a near‑zero **force** on the atom (since there is no bonding partner).
