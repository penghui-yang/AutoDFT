Determine the equilibrium spacing between graphene layers in bulk graphite (the lattice distance along the stacking, or “c‑axis,” direction) by computing how the total energy changes as you slightly expand or compress the layer separation. Graphite is a classic case where the layers are held together largely by long‑range van der Waals (dispersion) forces, which standard semilocal DFT tends to miss, leading to an unrealistically large interlayer distance. By including a physically motivated dispersion correction (Tkatchenko–Scheffler), the energy–vs–spacing curve should show a clear minimum at the optimal interlayer separation. The expected outcome is an equilibrium c‑axis spacing close to experiment (about 6.7 Å), rather than the much larger value predicted without dispersion.
