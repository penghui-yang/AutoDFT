Compute the local (atom-by-atom and orbital-resolved) electronic density of states for a clean ferromagnetic Ni(100) surface, using a slab model that represents a few nickel layers separated by vacuum. This targets how the electronic structure changes at a surface compared with “bulk-like” interior layers, which is important for understanding surface reactivity, catalysis, and magnetic behavior in transition metals. Because nickel is magnetic and its bonding is dominated by partially filled \(d\)-states, the surface is expected to show a narrowing of \(d\)-bands and an increased spin (exchange) splitting, leading to an enhanced magnetic moment at the outermost layers relative to the center of the slab. The main outputs should be spin-resolved, site- and orbital-projected DOS/LDOS for each layer (surface vs interior) and the corresponding layer-resolved charges and magnetic moments; this DOS analysis assumes you start from a previously relaxed Ni(100) surface structure.
