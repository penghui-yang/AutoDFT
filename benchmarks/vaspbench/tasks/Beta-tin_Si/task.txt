This calculation optimizes the crystal structure of silicon in the β‑tin phase, a high‑pressure metallic form that is structurally and electronically very different from normal diamond‑cubic silicon. The goal is to let both the atom positions and the full unit cell (its volume and shape) relax until the internal forces and stresses are minimized, revealing the equilibrium geometry favored by quantum‑mechanical bonding in this phase. This is useful for comparing the stability of β‑tin Si to other Si phases and for studying pressure‑driven phase transitions. The expected outcome is a fully relaxed lattice (cell parameters/shape) and relaxed atomic coordinates, along with the corresponding minimized total energy and residual stress that characterize the equilibrium β‑tin structure.
