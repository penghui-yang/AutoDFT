Calculate the electronic band gap of crystalline silicon in its diamond‑cubic bulk structure, and compare how different levels of electronic-structure theory predict this key semiconductor property. Silicon is a non‑magnetic, well‑characterized benchmark material whose experimentally known band gap makes it ideal for testing how much standard DFT (which typically underestimates gaps) improves when some exact Hartree–Fock–like exchange is included via hybrid functionals, or when using pure Hartree–Fock. The expected result from each method is a set of energy bands (or eigenvalues) from which you extract the band gap as the energy difference between the top of the valence band and the bottom of the conduction band. Because hybrid and Hartree–Fock–based approaches are harder to converge, it is commonly done as a follow‑up step starting from a well‑converged baseline DFT electronic structure.
