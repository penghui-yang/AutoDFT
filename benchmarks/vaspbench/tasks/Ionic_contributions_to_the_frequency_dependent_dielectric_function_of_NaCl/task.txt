Compute the full frequency‑dependent dielectric response of bulk rock‑salt NaCl, separating how the electron cloud polarizes at high frequencies (the “ion‑clamped” electronic dielectric function) from how the Na and Cl ions themselves move and create additional polarization at low frequencies (the lattice/ionic contribution). This is especially important for an ionic insulator like NaCl because infrared‑active optical phonons carry a strong dipole moment, so lattice vibrations can dominate the dielectric response near phonon frequencies and strongly increase the static dielectric constant. The expected results are (i) an electronic dielectric spectrum giving the high‑frequency dielectric constant \( \varepsilon_\infty \), (ii) an ionic dielectric spectrum derived from phonon modes and Born effective charges, and (iii) their sum \( \varepsilon(\omega)=\varepsilon_{\rm elec}(\omega)+\varepsilon_{\rm ion}(\omega) \), including the total static value \( \varepsilon_0=\varepsilon_\infty+\varepsilon_{\rm ion} \). Because this approach assumes an energy gap, it is appropriate for insulators and semiconductors like NaCl, but not for metals.
