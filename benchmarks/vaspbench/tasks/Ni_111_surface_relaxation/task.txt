Model a clean, close-packed Ni(111) surface as a thin slab separated by vacuum and allow only the top two atomic layers to relax while keeping the deeper layers fixed to mimic the bulk crystal. The goal is to find the equilibrium surface geometry (small inward/outward shifts of the top layers) and the corresponding minimum-energy surface structure, which is important because surface atomic positions control properties like catalytic activity, adsorption strength, and surface stability. From this calculation you should obtain the relaxed interlayer spacings and the final forces (near zero at convergence), and—when combined with a separate bulk Ni reference energy—the surface energy of Ni(111) to compare its stability against other facets such as Ni(100). In this example the system is treated without magnetism (spin polarization neglected), so the reported surface energy corresponds to a non-magnetic reference.
