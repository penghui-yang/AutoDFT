Compute the ground‑state energy and magnetic moment of an isolated oxygen atom using a spin‑polarized calculation, treating it as a single atom in a large “vacuum box” so it does not interact with periodic images. Oxygen is an open‑shell atom with unpaired electrons, so its lowest‑energy state breaks spherical symmetry and carries a net spin; if the simulation cell is too symmetric, the calculation can be artificially forced into a higher‑energy, overly symmetrized solution. By using a slightly non‑cubic (low‑symmetry) box, you allow the electronic density to relax into the true symmetry‑broken ground state. The expected result is a lower total energy and a magnetic moment close to 2 Bohr magnetons, confirming the correct open‑shell atomic state.
