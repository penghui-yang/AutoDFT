Simulate, at finite temperature, a gas-phase nucleophilic substitution reaction in which an incoming chloride anion approaches methyl chloride (CH₃Cl) and replaces the original chloride leaving group (an S\(_\mathrm{N}\)2-type process). The system is modeled as an isolated charged molecule–ion complex (net −1 charge), so the key physics is how thermal motion and electrostatic interactions drive bond breaking and bond forming between carbon and the two competing Cl atoms. The main output is a time series of the reaction-coordinate proxy defined as the difference between the two C–Cl distances (forming minus breaking bond), which should fluctuate near +1 Å for reactant-like configurations, near −1 Å for product-like configurations, and around 0 Å near the transition-state region. From this trajectory you then compute a histogram/probability distribution of that distance difference to quantify which configurations are most frequently sampled during the dynamics.
