Run a first-principles electronic-structure calculation on an isolated Fe–Fe dimer to determine its magnetic state while *forcing* each Fe atom’s local magnetic moment to point in chosen directions. This small system is a useful test case because transition-metal dimers can have multiple competing magnetic solutions (e.g., different alignments of the two atomic moments), and the self-consistent calculation may otherwise drift into whichever alignment is easiest to converge. The goal is to obtain the self-consistent total energy and the resulting atom-resolved magnetic moments for a specified constrained magnetic configuration, so you can compare the energetic preference of different relative moment directions (ferromagnetic vs. noncollinear arrangements). Because the constraint is enforced with an added “penalty” term, the output should also report the additional constraint energy so you can judge how closely the target moment directions were achieved.
